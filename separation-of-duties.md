# Separation of duties

Autonomy without separation is a faster way to ship an unauditable product. These principles are the operating constitution. The delivery stage uses the same constitution on a founder’s own software.

## 1. One writer, another acceptor

The role that proposes a change does not accept it.

The spec owner does not implement. The developer does not pass the slice. The coach does not commit the graph. The extractor does not speak for the product. The compiler does not grade the product it just materialized. A critic’s agreement does not waive a validator.

## 2. The founder has one desk

Decisions about meaning are made in one conversation. Other roles do not open a side chat and take a quiet “yes.”

Acceptance and defect work may show evidence and ask whether to continue or return to that desk. They do not renegotiate the product there.

## 3. A stop is real

A risk reviewer can refuse to start a hop. An acceptance operator can refuse to continue when the goal was missed or the result is doubtful. A deterministic constraint can refuse progression.

The implementer cannot clear those stops. Clearing a founder-level stop is itself a recorded decision.

## 4. Narrow roles beat a clever mode switch

If two jobs differ by mandate or by stop condition, they are two roles. A single agent that sometimes coaches, sometimes writes the graph, and sometimes deploys will do all three when it is proud of a partial answer.

The factory learned this the expensive way: a loose “implement this” became code, sometimes even working code, and the contract was never written. Working code without a contract is a failed run.

## 5. The exception has a price

A trivial defect may be fixed in the workshop without a full loop. That permission is a named role, not a mood.

Every use records why the wall was opened and how the rule gets tighter. If that sentence is missing, the fix is out of mandate. The exception is not allowed to become the normal path.

## 6. Language and arithmetic are different jobs

Models translate, converse, and draft. Deterministic services compute scores, check shape, bound invention, assemble context, migrate, deploy, and materialize.

A model is allowed to be wrong in a draft. It is not allowed to be the source of a number the rest of the system will treat as measured.

## 7. The preview is the commit

What the founder is shown is what save stores. A second hidden pass at save time is forbidden, because it would make the preview a suggestion.

Ambiguous new facts are withheld. They are not stored as a confident guess. The founder hears a question. The rest can still be saved.

## 8. Harvest, never the whole corpus

An agent receives the nodes, the depth, and the files its job needs. Giving it the entire archive feels thorough and produces two failures: cost, and attention spent on nuances that do not belong to this change.

The founder states the aim, the assumption, or the chosen option. Agents expand that into the full record, propagate it along relations, and close the contract for the other roles. The founder corrects assumptions, not paragraphs.

## 9. Isolation is in the loop

Two threads may proceed together when they do not share a file, a decision, or a gate.

Side branches were considered and declined for this research. The main line moves forward. Mistakes are caught by acceptance and by the next rule, not by rewriting history. Destructive undo is not a tool the agents hold.

## 10. Worlds do not share a database

The platform that stores the strategy graph is not the product a founder’s customers log into.

Separate data, separate secrets, separate runtimes. Sharing a machine in an early stage does not relax the wall. A secret may exist in the product runtime and, for a test, inside the acceptance run. It may not exist in a node, a prompt, a tool argument, or a log.

## 11. The founder is not a compiler

The founder is asked for decisions only a founder can make: empathy, unusual knowledge, risk appetite, hypotheses, secrets, spend, legal exposure, irreversible data, emergency stop.

The founder is not asked to certify migration safety or browser-test sufficiency they cannot realistically assess. Those belong to roles and to checks. Using the founder as a rubber stamp is a separation failure in the other direction.

## 12. Risk travels up, not down

Any agent may raise the risk class of an action. None may lower a class that a deterministic rule already assigned.

Production is one input to that class. It is not an automatic request for a human click.

## 13. A defect leaves knowledge

A hotfix that does not leave a rule, a contract, or a test is incomplete. The class of the mistake has to be harder to repeat than the instance was to patch.

Root cause outranks the patch. If the cause is still unknown, the loop continues. It does not get a green mark for a plausible story.

## 14. Autonomy is granted, then bounded

The factory is allowed to run for a long time without the founder, inside the decisions already made. That grant does not include secrets, another founder’s data, a rewrite of history, or a change of product meaning.

Policy can set spend, risk tolerance, providers, and how often to notify. Policy cannot hand an agent a secret or switch the validators off.

## 15. The same constitution, dogfooded

Zenonum was built by the workflow it exists to provide. The closed beta is the evidence that the loop can carry a product larger than a landing page, from strategy through human acceptance.

The delivery stage does not invent a looser constitution for code. It adds a compiler and an acceptance runner so the constitution can reach a running product line without making the model the deployer.
